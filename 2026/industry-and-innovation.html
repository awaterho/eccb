    Industry and innovation session

    Join a dedicated session showcasing scientific innovations and research advancements from industry. Invited speakers present recent technological developments and share perspectives on how industry is shaping the future of computational biology and the life sciences.

    Time Activity
    14:50 - 15:10
    Beyond FAIR: Making life-science data AI-ready and responsibly governed | Frontiers

    Speakers : Sean Hill, Frontiers & Thomas Collin (Bioinformatics: Frontiers in Bioinformatics, Systems biology: Frontiers in Systems Biology)

    Computational biology generates and deposits data at remarkable scale, yet much of it is far harder to reuse than its presence in a repository suggests. A sequencing run, a single-cell object, a folder of microscopy images and a methods file may all satisfy a deposit mandate, but reusing them in practice still depends on tacit knowledge held by the producing lab, because the metadata needed to interpret them is incomplete. As foundation models become central to the life sciences, the stakes rise: AI performance in biology rests on data quality, readiness and governance. Closing the gap between data that is merely findable and data that is genuinely AI-ready is now a central challenge for reproducibility and discovery.

    This talk presents FAIR² Data Management as a response to that challenge. FAIR² extends the FAIR principles with two further dimensions: AI Readiness (AIR), data that machines can ingest, interpret and learn from; and Responsible AI, provenance, governance and accountability carried with the data, so that what models learn from can be traced and trusted. Clara, an AI data steward, works with the diverse and complex data that life-science research generates and turns a single submission into four peer-reviewed outputs: a citable Data Article, an interactive data Portal, an AI-ready data package, and a FAIR² certificate, with lifetime hosting, far faster than manual curation allows.

    The journals hosting this session reflect precisely the research culture FAIR² is designed to serve. Frontiers in Systems Biology, led by Prof. Yoram Vodovotz, breaks down the silos of modern research to promote integration and systems thinking across disciplines. Frontiers in Bioinformatics, led by Prof. Adam Godzik, provides an innovative forum for new bioinformatics discoveries, championing the tools and applications that bring computational insight to biological problems. Both journals advance open, reproducible science, and both accelerate discovery when the datasets underpinning their published research are not merely deposited but genuinely reusable and AI-ready.

    Looking ahead, we make the case for a future in which every dataset is a first-class research object: peer-reviewed, openly citable, machine-actionable and responsibly governed by design. The goal is not only to close the reproducibility gap, but to build a data foundation for AI in the life sciences that the whole community can build on and trust.

    15:10 - 15:15
    From structure to function: geometric deep learning for engineering biomolecular interactions | Lemna Bio

    Speaker: Lucien Krapp, Lemna Bio

    Geometric deep learning significantly increases the accuracy of interaction analysis and biomolecular engineering, pushing frontiers in medicine, biotech, and sustainability. Building on these advances, this talk introduces PeSTo for predicting binding interfaces, and CARBonAra for context-aware design across diverse functions, from binders and enzymes to RNA design.

    15:15 - 15:25
    Assessing targets with AI agents | Roche

    Speaker: Fabian Birzele, Roche

    Drug R&D is a high-stakes, multi-stage process where early errors often compound, leading to high failure rates in clinical translation. Addressing this requires a shift in how scientific decisions are made across the discovery continuum. This presentation introduces Target Nexus, an AI-powered platform designed to enhance scientific rigor and transparency in target discovery. By integrating agentic AI with internal proprietary data, Target Nexus acts as a co-analyst that bridges the gap between scientific hypotheses and audit-ready governance recommendations. The platform emphasizes a human-in-the-loop approach, ensuring that every AI-generated conclusion remains under scientist control and is traceable to primary sources. With a modular architecture that continuously learns from proprietary experiments and governance outcomes, Target Nexus moves beyond static applications, providing a dynamic system that evolves with organizational expertise. The project demonstrates the potential of interdisciplinary team science to transform data into informed decisions, ultimately aiming to increase the success and speed of drug discovery.

    15:25 - 15:35
    Intelligence that Scales: Powerful Agents. Reproducible Workflows | seqera

    Speaker: Jon Manning, seqera

    Pipeline development in pharma and biotech doesn't have the luxury of "good enough." Workflows have to be reproducible and auditable, while still keeping pace with the speed AI-driven discovery demands. This talk will showcase how the latest features of Seqera Platform, including Co-Scientist and Intelligent Compute, are changing the way scientists build, run, and scale bioinformatics, without compromising reproducibility.

    15:35 - 15:40
    A programmer walks into a biology lab | smarts.bio

    Speaker: Nir Orpaz, smarts.bio

    Software engineers have tools and habits they'd never give up - version control, fast iteration, the freedom to try things and see what breaks. Biology rarely imported any of them. Yet the tools biologists need often already exist, from image analysis to models like Boltz-2, just locked behind cost, setup, and friction. A computer scientist's short stories from the lab on what changes (and what becomes possible) when you take the friction away.

    Exploring digital twins for indication and patient selection | iOnctura

    Entrepreneurs' stories session

    Wednesday 2 September 2026, 10:30 - 11:30

    Join a dedicated session showcasing 

    Speaker Title
    Samir Ounzain | CEO and co-founder of Haya Therapeutics
    From curiosity to company: Discovering hidden biology and building new medicines

    Samir Ounzain, Ph.D., is a molecular biologist, entrepreneur, and co-founder and CEO of HAYA Therapeutics. His work has focused on unlocking the largely unexplored regulatory genome and translating that biology into new therapeutic approaches.

    Before founding HAYA, Samir was a project leader at Lausanne University Hospital (CHUV), where he discovered and characterized novel long non-coding RNAs in cardiovascular disease, including WISPER, which became the foundation of HAYA’s lead program.

    At HAYA, he is building a new class of precision RNA medicines that integrate computational biology, functional genomics and RNA therapeutics to identify disease-driving regulatory programs and reprogram pathological cell states. Under his leadership, HAYA has advanced its first program into clinical development and formed strategic collaborations, including with Eli Lilly and Company.

    Samir’s broader vision is to unite scientific curiosity, computational innovation, and emerging biology to uncover hidden disease mechanisms, define causal biology, and enable cellular reprogramming approaches that expand human healthspan and create new classes of medicines.

    He has been recognized as an emerging leader in biotechnology and life sciences, including In Vivo’s 30 Rising Leaders in Life Sciences (2026), Pharmaceutical Executive’s Emerging Pharma Leaders (2025), and The Medicine Maker Power List (2025), as well as the Andreas & Thomas Strüngmann Award for life-science entrepreneurship.

    Jurgi Camblong | Founder & Executive Chairman of Sophia Genetics
    A journey in data driven medicine, from research to entrepreneurship 

    Dr. Jurgi Camblong, Ph.D., M.B.A., is a visionary entrepreneur who is pioneering the data-driven medicine movement around the world.

    In 2011, he founded SOPHiA GENETICS with Dr. Pierre Hutter and Prof. Lars Steinmetz and has successfully led the development of the company as CEO ever since. Jurgi was a member of the Advisory Council on Digital Transformation to the Swiss Government, is a board member of the Swiss Biotech Association and was personally recognized by EY as Emergent Entrepreneur of the Year in 2017, listed among the 50 Smartest Companies by the MIT Technology Review. Jurgi has been a member of the board of directors since March 2011.

    He is a molecular biologist (published in Cell, Science and Nature), has a Ph.D. in life sciences from the University of Geneva and an Executive M.B.A. in management of technology from EPFL/HEC Lausanne.

    About SIB

    The SIB Swiss Institute of Bioinformatics is an internationally recognized non-profit organization, dedicated to biological and biomedical data science. Its data scientists are passionate about creating knowledge and solving complex questions in many fields, from biodiversity and evolution to medicine. They provide essential databases and software platforms as well as bioinformatics expertise and services to academic, clinical, and industry groups. SIB federates the Swiss bioinformatics community of some 800 scientists, encouraging collaboration and knowledge sharing.
